## 2. The conceptual answer (does the model collapse to prob×cost?)

No, but the author is half right and that half is deliberate:

- **The counterparty leg IS prob×cost** (`ℓ_C = h·ξ`, from market proxies) and is kept reduced-form on purpose.
- **The depeg leg cannot be reduced**, for three reasons: (a) the realised price `p^sec(D)` is endogenous to the run size, not a transportable market prior; (b) the premium is an integral over a continuum of partial-conversion states, and the tail run is only ~5% of the USDC premium (ℓ_{D,s} weighted = 2.6 of 48.9), so a binary run model misses the bulk; (c) the payment-holder haircut must be separated from the market-maker's realised price (the 0.902 trough is the intermediary's, not the holder's).
- **Punchline:** the reduction is approximately right for USDT (84% counterparty) and approximately wrong for USDC (54% baseline depeg). A single reduced-form recipe would misprice one of the two coins, which is exactly the differentiated-regulation thesis. The complexity is what reveals that a reduced form fails on USDC.
