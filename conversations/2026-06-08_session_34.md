## 2. On-chain fetch (Ethereum, Etherscan V2)

`simulation/scripts/fetch_onchain_redemptions.py` pulled ERC-20 mint (Transfer from 0x0) and burn (Transfer to 0x0) events for USDC and USDT over the two stress windows, aggregated to hourly net redemption. Results (`data/processed/onchain_redemptions_*.csv`, `onchain_redemptions_summary.json`):

- **USDC, SVB window (8--18 Mar 2023):** 336 mints, 1,914 burns, $8.80bn burned, $2.40bn minted, **net redemption $6.40bn**, across 197 active hours. Rich series.
- **USDT, Terra window (1--26 May 2022):** **0 mints, 2 burns, $917 total** -- Ethereum captures essentially none of USDT's Terra redemptions (they were on Tron).

## 3. Hourly regression and the timing finding

Merging the hourly USDC on-chain net redemption with the hourly secondary depeg over the SVB window gives **no contemporaneous relationship** (corr $-0.06$, $R^2<0.01$, slightly negative). The reason is economic, not a data problem: during SVB, Circle's primary redemption was **suspended over the weekend** (banking closed; Coinbase paused conversions), so the secondary price crashed to 0.90 on 11--12 March precisely while on-chain burns were constrained; the large at-par redemptions (burns) surged only after the federal backstop restored the peg on 13 March. Contemporaneous hourly mint/burn is therefore the wrong regressor; the daily and threshold mechanism checks (which work) capture the episode-level relationship.

## 4. Tron verdict

Reported to the author: **not worth pursuing Tron now.** It is feasible (Tronscan has a free API), but (i) the Ethereum exercise showed high-frequency contemporaneous mint/burn does not improve the regression because of the redemption-suspension lead-lag, which would apply equally to USDT; (ii) the USDT/Terra depeg was mild (~250 bps vs USDC's 978); (iii) re-tooling the pipeline for Tron is real work for a low expected payoff. Keep the daily + threshold mechanism check; a lead-lag reframe (not more chains) would be the route if we wanted to push the on-chain angle.

## 5. Descriptive figure (the productive use of the data)

The author asked whether the on-chain data could still add something descriptive. It can, and it is a strong exhibit: `make_fig_svb_onchain.py` -> `paper/figures/fig_svb_onchain.pdf`, a dual-axis chart of the USDC secondary price (with the below-par depeg shaded) against hourly on-chain redemptions over the SVB window, with event markers (SVB closed / Circle disclosure; backstop / redemption reopens). It shows the depeg opening while primary redemption is constrained and the at-par burns surging only after the peg recovers. Added as Figure~\ref{fig:svb_onchain} in §5.4 with a framing paragraph: the holder's loss is the secondary discount borne while par redemption is unavailable, the object the global game prices, distinct from and preceding the orderly redemptions that follow.
