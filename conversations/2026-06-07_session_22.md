## 2. The two conceptual clarifications (author's background questions)

- **Two distinct endogeneities.** (A) The coordination fixed point `F(s*)=E[V_R(D(s*))−V_H]=0` ties the *marginal* holder's indifference to the *aggregate* conversion mass — the self-fulfilling run; this is the global game and it was always present. (B) Cross-risk coupling ties the counterparty channel to the depeg channel. These are different objects; (A) is the run, (B) is the new interaction the author asked for.
- **Relation to the Fed FEDS 2026-037 paper.** Their "one risk reinforces the other" is the *coordination* reinforcement (more runners → worse outcome → more runners), which our depeg channel already has via (A). Our finding that counterparty and depeg price *additively* is a statement about cross-*source* coupling (B), a different thing — so there is no contradiction: our exercise does not violate their premise, it locates the fragility in the coordination run and reports that the second, cross-source coupling is second-order at the calibrated parameters.

## 3. The correction implemented

- **Removed the double-count.** `−ξ·h` deleted from the realised secondary price (`secondary.py`, `payoffs.py` `raw_price`, and the `p^{sec}` equation in `B_formal_model.tex`).
- **Counterparty now enters `V_H`.** `V_H = θ − c_q q + ω − ξ·h`, so the counterparty loss lowers the value of holding, raises the conversion mass, and feeds the coordination channel — i.e. it actually moves the threshold instead of cancelling. (`payoffs.py`, `eq:VH_app` in `B_formal_model.tex`, with an added paragraph explaining why placement in `V_H` is the substantive correction.)
