## 2. Idea list delivered (collaborator mode, advisory)

A ranked list of proactive extensions was offered, each with a way-forward and a rough effort/payoff read:

1. **Coupling wedge** — let the issuer counterparty hazard enter the holders' conversion decision (state-dependent hazard) so counterparty and depeg risk interact rather than add; measure the wedge. *Tested below.*
2. **State-dependent / regime-switching stress probability** — endogenise `p_s` on observable stress indicators.
3. **External validation: structural vs market-implied premium** — back a market-implied premium out of observed stablecoin prices and compare to the structural `Π_risk`. *Tested below.*
4. **Cross-section beyond USDC/USDT** — extend the calibration to a third issuer (e.g. PYUSD/DAI) to populate the per-issuer risk map.
5. **Off-ramp basis micro-data** — replace the approximate destination basis with a measured panel.

## 3. Sandbox test of idea 3 (market-implied validation)

A throwaway script (`sandbox_market_implied.py`, **not** added to the paper, not in the repo deliverables) backed a crude market-implied premium out of the observed secondary-price distribution and compared it to the structural decomposition. Finding: the exercise is directionally consistent but under-identified from price data alone without an options/term-structure instrument the dataset does not contain; recorded as a way-forward, not pursued now (consistent with the "no missing-data findings" ruling).

## 4. Sandbox test of idea 1 (coupling wedge) — the consequential one

A second throwaway script (`sandbox_coupling_wedge.py`) made the counterparty hazard feed the conversion decision and measured the interaction. **The test surfaced a modelling bug rather than a clean wedge:** in the existing code the counterparty term `−ξ·h` was subtracted inside the *realised secondary price*, where it is common to both redeeming and holding and therefore mechanical — the equilibrium rate was invariant to `h`, so counterparty risk was being **double-counted** (once in the standalone `ℓ_C` term and once, inertly, in the price). The coupling the author wanted could not exist in that specification because the term sat in the wrong place.

## 5. Outcome / handoff to session 22

The sandbox exploration established (a) idea 3 is a way-forward needing an instrument we lack, and (b) idea 1 exposed a double-count that must be fixed: counterparty risk belongs in the value of *holding* `V_H`, not in the realised price. This set up the correction implemented in session 22. No paper or model-source files were changed in this session; the two sandbox scripts are scratch and were flagged for deletion.
