## 3. Assessment delivered to the author

**Fed (Fragility of Perfectly Safe Digital Money).** Closest methodological sibling. Same global-game / Morris-Shin coordination engine, unique redemption threshold, regime-switching jump — i.e. the author's "if good very good, if bad very unstable." Their two reinforcing forces are **network externalities** (adoption raises value) vs **congestion / gas fees** (use raises cost); their interaction produces strategic complementarities and runs **even with perfectly safe reserves**. **Key difference / our niche:** their signal is over **congestion γ (the rail)**, abstracting from reserve risk by design; the paper's signal is over the **fundamental θ (the liability: counterparty + depeg)**, which it prices as a premium and decomposes, with congestion entering as a cost component. So the two papers are complementary on different axes — they price the rail fragility, the paper prices the liability premium. Both run a public-rail cost counterfactual (they: Fedwire-on-Ethereum, gas an order of magnitude above and far more volatile; the paper: PIX/FedNow/Nexus/Wise/SWIFT) and both document the USDT Ethereum→Tron migration under congestion (already in the paper's UC3/chain-choice). Authors overlap with the already-cited Gorton et al. (2022). **Must cite**, with an explicit liability-vs-rail positioning paragraph.

**BIS WP 1355 (Making stablecoins stable(r): can regulation help?).** Dynamic fiat-backed issuer with AR(1) flow shocks (matches the paper's weekly flow persistence), bond fire-sales (Shleifer-Vishny), endogenous coin-holder discipline via regulator-set thresholds. Two regulatory tools — liquidity-ratio (LR) and capital-ratio (CR) thresholds as *usable buffers* — mapped two-ways to two policy targets: PD (default, micro) and EPI (expected price impact, macro). LR and CR are substitutes for either target but complements for joint PD+EPI. **No cross-stablecoin heterogeneity.** **Our value-add:** the inverted USDC (depeg/liquidity-dominant) vs USDT (counterparty-dominant) decomposition makes the optimal LR-vs-CR mix coin-specific — capital/CR bites the counterparty-dominant USDT, liquidity/secondary-depth bites the depeg-dominant USDC. This bridges the paper's positive measurement to their normative toolkit and grounds the differentiated-regulation argument.
