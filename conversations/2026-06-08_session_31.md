It makes three points: (i) a stablecoin bundles a means of payment with a rail, whereas the public/incumbent/fintech alternatives are rails moving a different asset (a commercial-bank deposit, par-redeemable, settled in central-bank money); (ii) the rail's operational risk is common and nets out, so what survives the comparison is the asset the payer briefly holds; (iii) on the non-stablecoin rails that asset is backstopped by deposit insurance, central-bank settlement, and orderly resolution, so the retail payer bears no counterparty or depeg risk and the risk column is zero, with no system-level deposit run to calibrate precisely because the backstops prevented one.
