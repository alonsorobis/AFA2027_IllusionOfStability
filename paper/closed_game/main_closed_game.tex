\begin{abstract}
\noindent Sending a fiat-backed stablecoin costs a fraction of a correspondent-banking payment, but the fee understates the cost the sender bears: the holder is exposed to issuer counterparty risk and to secondary-market price dispersion when par convertibility breaks. We price the resulting expected loss in two components: a counterparty term read from the issuer's reserve quality, and a depeg term priced through a global game of holder coordination, calibrated separately to the March 2023 USDC and May 2022 USDT episodes, obtaining 49 and 71 basis points with inverted compositions. Across three corridors the risk-adjusted stablecoin path is dominated where a public instant-payment rail exists, wins where none does, and is absorbed once an upgraded public cross-border rail goes live. Pricing this cost, and recognising that it differs by issuer, reframes two central-bank decisions: whether to build or license cross-border infrastructure, and whether one rulebook can supervise instruments whose risks are composed so differently.
\end{abstract}
